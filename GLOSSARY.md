# Learning platform

A general-purpose learning product for courses across different subjects, audiences, and experience levels.

## Language

**Learning platform**:
The product through which learners access courses and educational materials, follow learning paths, practice, and track their learning. Its scope spans multiple subjects and experience levels.

**Handbook**:
The Node.js Backend Engineering Handbook, the platform's first educational content source and initial use case.

**Course**:
A structured educational offering with its own learning outcomes, organized into sections containing lessons. A course can be discovered and accessed independently of learning paths and can belong to multiple paths.

**Section**:
A grouping of related lessons within a course.

**Lesson**:
A course-specific learning unit that can combine multiple educational materials into a learning step. Reusing its materials elsewhere does not share its completion, learner progress, assessment results, or learning outcomes across courses.

**Educational material**:
Content or an activity supporting a lesson, such as text, video, images, code examples, interactive content, or exercises. A material can be reused across courses without becoming a separate lesson, catalog entry, or enrollment requirement.

**Course sequence**:
The defined ordering of sections and lessons within a course. The ordering alone does not impose prerequisites or mandatory linear progression.

**Learning path**:
A structured journey connecting multiple courses toward a broader educational goal, with each course retaining its own internal structure and course sequence. Its ordering alone does not require mandatory linear progression.

**Enrollment**:
A learner's choice to add a course to their ongoing studies and track progress. Enrollment does not itself confer access entitlement, imply payment, or require completion.

**Access entitlement**:
Permission for a learner to access particular educational content, separate from enrollment.

**Learning-path participation**:
A learner's explicit choice to follow a learning path, organizing qualifying course progress toward its broader goal. Participation does not create duplicate course progress or automatically enroll the learner in its courses.

**Course archiving**:
A learner's choice to remove an enrolled course from active studies without canceling enrollment, independently changing access entitlement, or deleting learning history.

**Prerequisite**:
Prior knowledge, skill, or learning experience identified as preparation for a learning step. A prerequisite provides guidance unless an explicit progression restriction makes it mandatory.

**Progression restriction**:
An explicitly declared requirement that limits access to a learning step for an educational reason. It must explain both the requirement and its rationale.

**Required lesson**:
A lesson designated as necessary for course completion.

**Optional lesson**:
A lesson offered as enrichment that is not required for course completion.

**Required course**:
A course designated as necessary for completion of a particular learning path.

**Optional course**:
A course offered as enrichment within a particular learning path that is not required for that path's completion.

**Learner activity**:
A learner's participation and interactions during learning. Activity alone does not establish completion or demonstrated competency.

**Learner progress**:
A learner's recorded advancement through a structured educational journey. It is distinct from the learning outcomes of a course and does not by itself demonstrate competency.

**Completion**:
Fulfillment of explicitly defined learning requirements for a lesson, course, or learning path. Completion alone does not establish demonstrated competency.

**Current requirement fulfillment**:
The extent to which a learner's completion and evidence satisfy the requirements currently published for a lesson, course, or learning path. It is distinct from historical completion under earlier requirements.

**Historical completion**:
A preserved record that a learner fulfilled the requirements applicable to a lesson, course, or learning path at a particular time. It does not automatically establish fulfillment of revised current requirements.

**Demonstrated competency**:
A learner's ability to apply relevant knowledge or skills, established through appropriate evidence.

**Practice**:
An opportunity for a learner to develop knowledge or skills through an activity aligned with course learning outcomes.

**Assessment**:
Evaluation of a learner's performance against defined criteria aligned with course learning outcomes.

**Assessment attempt**:
A learner's individual undertaking of an assessment, with its associated submission, evaluation result, and feedback as applicable. Submission alone is distinct from passing.

**Reassessment**:
Evaluation requiring additional evidence to establish fulfillment of revised learning requirements. It is distinct from retrying an assessment under unchanged requirements.

**Feedback**:
Guidance that helps a learner understand their performance and improve their knowledge or skills.

**Editorial approval**:
A judgment that educational content is ready for publication. It is separate from individual learner progress, completion, and demonstrated competency.

**Author**:
A responsibility for preparing and revising educational content. The same person may also act as publisher.

**Publisher**:
A responsibility for reviewing, approving, publishing, and withdrawing educational content. It does not require a different person from the author.

**Draft**:
Educational content or changes being prepared for publication, unavailable to learners until explicitly published.

**Published course version**:
A coherent, reviewed course structure and its selected educational material revisions made available together through publication.

**Publication recovery**:
Explicit republication of a previously approved course version as a new publication event, subject to current publication eligibility and access restrictions.

**Course retirement**:
Withdrawal of a course from new discovery and enrollment while preserving access for existing learners and retaining historical learning records.

**Material retirement**:
Withdrawal of an educational material from selection for future publications while preserving its existing published uses.

**Access removal**:
Making affected educational content unavailable when necessary, with a recorded reason. It does not automatically delete historical learning records.

**Course learning outcome**:
What a learner is intended to know or be able to do through a particular course. Learning outcomes belong to individual courses rather than defining the platform's subject or audience.

**Self-paced learning**:
Independent study in which learners choose when to start, continue, and progress through a course without depending on a fixed schedule or instructor availability.
