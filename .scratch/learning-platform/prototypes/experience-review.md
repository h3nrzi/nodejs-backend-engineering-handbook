# Stitch experience references and scope review

Decision: [Validate the first-release learner and publisher experience](../issues/07-first-release-experience-prototype.md#answer).

The owner accepts this ten-screen Google Stitch collection as the temporary visual prototype for product discovery. No further visual polishing or replacement interactive prototype is required. The earlier Codex interactive prototype was intentionally deleted. Its previously approved behavioral decisions and technical follow-ups are preserved in the decision ticket; earlier instructions to finish reviewing that deleted artifact are superseded.

These are static visual references. Each screen includes a PNG and an HTML export. Some exports contain small presentation/demo handlers, but they do not establish working end-user journeys, persistence, responsive behavior, keyboard/focus behavior, accessibility compliance, publication enforcement, or actual learner validation. Acceptance of these references settles product-discovery direction, not implementation or pilot acceptance.

## Scope precedence and meaningful gaps

The approved [release boundary](../issues/06-first-release-and-phases.md#answer), [learner rules](../issues/04-learner-experience-and-evidence.md#answer), [publishing rules](../issues/03-authoring-and-publishing.md#answer) and [experience resolution](../issues/07-first-release-experience-prototype.md#answer) govern interpretation. The following differences are recorded for specification and implementation; they do not initiate another design iteration or add release capabilities.

| Observation in the supplied references | First-release interpretation and remaining obligation |
| --- | --- |
| Certificates, downloadable diploma PDFs, validated competency, grades, exams/quizzes and final assessed projects appear across My learning, course/path details, archive, revisions, invitation and design notes. Some copy guarantees skills after completion. | Exclude certificate issuance, assessed submissions, grading/reviewer workflows and competency claims. Outcomes express educational intent; learner-confirmed completion does not establish skill. Required written practice must have a supported learner-confirmation route. Carry explicit exclusions into the spec. |
| Locked later lessons/courses, prerequisite-completion labels and readiness/final-evaluation language appear in the reader, paths and design notes. | Sequence and preparation are advisory. No mandatory prerequisite verification or educational progression lock ships. A course not yet enrolled is a learner choice, distinct from access denial, retirement or unavailable content. |
| The reader advertises an audio version; interruption recovery depicts unavailable video, an assessment blocked on video, PDF fallback, local-first storage and automatic offline synchronization. Archive depicts downloadable ZIP/PDF attachments and personal notes/bookmarks. | Initial materials remain formatted text, images, code examples and written practice instructions. These references do not select audio/video delivery, downloads, executable activities, notes/bookmark persistence or offline/local-first functionality. Do not introduce an unsupported fulfillment dependency. Choose persistence and recovery contracts in the technical decision. |
| Retry/sync buttons, diagnostic messages and badges claim local storage, server acknowledgment, zero loss or guaranteed stability. | Treat these as illustrative copy only. Preserve the distinction between confirmed progress, uncertain outcomes and unavailability. Establish actual evidence, safe reconciliation and duplicate-effect prevention before displaying definitive results. Do not promise lossless storage or durable offline queues from these screens. |
| Archive, invitation and revision copy promises lifetime retention/access, perpetual availability, signed certificates or immutable academic records. | Archiving preserves enrollment/history and removes active recommendations; restoration uses current published requirements. Retirement preserves existing enrollees' access subject to active access-removal rules. Retention, deletion and account lifecycle policies remain unresolved technical/product contracts; no perpetual-access or unlimited-retention promise is approved. |
| Invitation/access and publishing share a sheet, and a “چرخه انتشار” navigation item appears in several exports. The publishing diagram assumes two senior reviewers, GPG signatures, Git hashes/branches and OAuth2 diagnostics. | Learner invitation/access is separate from author/publisher responsibilities. Use the [separate repository publisher walkthrough](publisher-workflow.md). No publisher dashboard/editor is required. One person may author and approve; two reviewers are not mandatory. Exact tools, signing, authorization and audit mechanisms remain open; no protocol is selected by the mockup. Publication is availability inside the admitted pilot, not authorization for public registration or rollout. |
| The revisions page says adopting the new version is optional and discusses upgrading a credential. | Current fulfillment always reflects latest published requirements; acknowledging changes is not an opt-in version switch. Preserve historical completion, explain new work, and keep the explanation accessible after acknowledgment. Explicit publisher equivalence decisions govern prior completion; no credential upgrade or learner-facing old-version selector is required. |
| Some dashboards emphasize percentages, hours or total completed units; optional course counts and status labels vary. Detailed pages use local content-section navigation. | Keep the three approved global areas and enrollment wording. Course progress counts fulfilled required lessons; path progress counts fulfilled required courses. Optional work, historical completion and obstacles are separate; time/percentages do not imply effort remaining or competency. Labels and controls must implement the approved behavior rather than every sample metric. |
| Catalog/path examples include software, architecture, product management and an econometrics interruption example, with invented course counts, learner cohorts, identities, dates and academic institutions. | These show representative subject variety, not actual available courses, approved curriculum, an institution, a changed pilot cohort or validation evidence. A bounded reviewed Handbook course may start the invited pilot. A contrasting course and credible path of at least two courses still require real validation before broader public rollout. |
| Core screens show many happy/exception states but not every loading, confirmed failure, reconciled success, changed saved position, retired required path course or cross-course access-removal state. Support is sometimes a mentor/FAQ link; privacy and no-invitation/account recovery are incomplete. | Existing decisions define these behaviors; finish their testable states and permissions in the technical/specification tickets. Use the dedicated support email without promising assessment support or a response time. Finalize actual support address, access/recovery, privacy disclosures, retention/deletion and eligibility contracts before operation. These are coverage obligations, not requests for more mockups. |

No gap above requires a new product decision to accept these temporary references: each is constrained by an existing approved decision or assigned to technical/specification work. If later work exposes a genuinely new policy choice, surface it explicitly rather than treating Stitch placeholder copy as authority.

## Publisher workflow is separate

The lower publishing section of the invitation/access sheet is an explanatory reference only. It neither grants publisher authority to invited learners nor creates a learner-facing publisher dashboard. The [repository publisher walkthrough](publisher-workflow.md) describes preview, review, exact-version approval, publication, equivalence, withdrawal and recovery independently of pilot admission and enrollment.
