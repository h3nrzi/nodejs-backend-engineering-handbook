## Question

What estimated operating costs and owner-approved spending limit make the selected first-release scope feasible under the conditionally accepted planning arrangement?

Estimate a manageable service combination for the selected release, using explicit workload, storage, delivery, and usage assumptions appropriate to an initial pilot of approximately 5–10 participants. That cohort size is provisional, not a guaranteed minimum or capacity limit. Include applicable hosting, persistence, content delivery, account-access services, backups, monitoring, support tooling, and other actual dependencies without assuming every category needs a separate paid service.

Create scoped research tickets for current prices, availability, eligibility, and limits once candidate services are concrete. Separate recurring costs, one-time costs, included allowances, usage-sensitive charges, and growth exposure; state estimate currency, date, and uncertainty. Cost candidate solutions without treating them as approved provider or architecture selections. Confirm that assumptions can support the approved learner and publisher experience.

Ask the owner to approve a monthly spending limit and how usage or cost overruns will be controlled. No amount has already been approved. Prioritize low operational complexity within the owner's provisional 10–15 hours per week; AI-agent assistance does not establish additional human operating capacity. If no feasible combination fits the accepted limit, explicitly reopen release scope or the budget rather than silently changing either. No purchase or deployment is authorized by this decision.

The costed options and approved limit constrain [Decide the first-release technical boundaries and feasibility](08-first-release-technical-boundaries.md). If detailed technical contracts expose materially different costs, reopen this decision before finalizing those choices.
