## Question

Do the phased blueprint and first-release specification express one consistent, testable product contract with no unresolved decisions blocking implementation?

Reconcile the resolved tickets into linked planning assets: the product blueprint and the first-release spec. Trace every included journey to its behavior, relevant data and content contracts, permissions, failure and recovery cases, acceptance criteria, and release validation approach. Check coverage against the agreed learner outcome, publisher workflow, operating constraints, and technical boundaries. Link the decision tickets rather than copying their full rationale. Surface remaining questions as tickets instead of assuming answers; resolve this readiness decision only through review with the owner. Implementation and actual launch remain outside this map.


Apply the scope and four-phase direction in [Define the first release and later product phases](06-first-release-and-phases.md#answer). Keep initial pilot readiness separate from broader public rollout: a bounded, reviewed Handbook course can support the initial invited pilot, while subject independence, multi-course behavior, and a credible path connecting at least two courses require validation before public rollout. Include the task session, return visit, follow-up, support-email readiness, and recording of assistance, failures, accessibility obstacles, and feedback in the evaluation plan. Owner review and automated checks do not substitute for actual learner findings or prove educational effectiveness. The planning handoff specifies these future evidence gates without requiring production implementation, content production, or an actual pilot within this map.

### Experience reference and validation coverage

Use [Validate the first-release learner and publisher experience](07-first-release-experience-prototype.md#answer) as the resolved experience contract and its [Stitch asset inventory/scope review](../prototypes/experience-review.md) as the accepted temporary visual reference. Preserve the [separate repository publisher workflow](../prototypes/publisher-workflow.md); learner invitation/access does not imply publishing authority, and no publisher dashboard is required. Do not reinstate the intentionally deleted interactive prototype or require another visual-polishing iteration.

Trace each meaningful screen gap/override into the implementation-ready specification: unsupported assessment/certificate/competency features, progression locks, deferred formats and offline guarantees, lifetime retention/access promises, unselected reviewer/signing/authentication mechanisms, and incomplete exception/support/privacy states must not silently become release commitments. Approved behavior takes precedence over sample screen copy. Capture technical outcomes from the prerequisite decision rather than inventing mechanisms here.

Specify future acceptance checks through actual end-user controls for discovery/enrollment, study/completion/undo, independent path participation, shared progress, archive/restore, revisions/history, unavailable work and safely reconciled uncertain writes. Include persistence and duplicate-effect checks, repository publication authorization/audit/recovery verification, responsive/mobile inspection, keyboard navigation, focus behavior and accessibility criteria. Static screenshots, HTML presentation handlers, and earlier model/source review pass none of these checks. Record actual results separately when implementation exists; their future execution does not block closing this planning map once the contracts and evidence gates are complete.

Retain actual pilot validation and broader-rollout multi-course/path validation from the release decision. Owner acceptance of visual references is discovery evidence, not learner usability, operating readiness or demonstrated educational effectiveness. This ticket remains open pending its existing prerequisite and owner review of the completed blueprint/specification.
