# Choose the first learner segment and learning outcome

Status: resolved
Type: grilling
Labels: wayfinder:grilling
Parent: [Chart the multi-course learning platform](../map.md)
Blocked by: none

## Question

Which learner segment should the first release serve, what learning problem will it solve for that segment, and what observable outcome would make that release useful?

Compare plausible segments across the intended course portfolio using the owner's access to learners, their unmet needs, available educational material, and ways to validate demand. Distinguish evidence from assumptions and identify any validation needed before committing. Determine how the Handbook can test the chosen proposition without defining the platform's identity or automatically restricting its audience to backend engineers.

## Comments

### Owner discovery — audience and proposition

- The owner chose people who want to learn through structured online courses, across subjects and experience levels. The first release is not limited to aspiring backend developers.
- The first-release priorities are discovering a course, accessing structured lessons, following a learning path, and tracking progress.
- The platform's primary outcome is enabling learners to follow and make measurable progress through a structured educational journey. Specific learning outcomes belong to individual courses.
- The Handbook is the initial course and content source for evaluating the learning experience; it does not define the platform's audience or subject boundaries.
- No reachable learner group or verified evidence of learner difficulties has been established. Learner needs remain hypotheses, and real-learner validation remains open.
- These initial answers were followed by the owner's explicit approval of independent, self-paced learning as the first-release context. The resolution below records the combined decision.

## Answer

### Audience and learning context

The first release serves people who want to learn through structured online courses, across subjects and experience levels. The owner deliberately chose a broad audience defined by its learning context rather than a narrower subject, career stage, or programming prerequisite. This is a product direction, not a validated market segment.

Independent, self-paced learning is the primary context. Learners should be able to discover and enroll in a course, access structured lessons, follow a learning path, study at their own pace, resume where they left off, and track progress without depending on a fixed schedule or instructor availability.

### Platform outcome and course outcomes

The platform enables learners to follow and make measurable progress through a structured educational journey. An observable evaluation scenario is a learner finding and enrolling in a course, studying part of it, leaving, and returning to continue with their progress visible. This scenario translates the owner's requested capabilities into a planning reference; it is not a reported learner test or a fully specified acceptance criterion.

Specific learning outcomes belong to individual courses. Learner progress does not by itself demonstrate competency. The meanings of enrollment, learning path, progress, and completion, and the evidence and measures supporting them, remain decisions for [Define courses, educational materials, and learning paths](02-course-and-learning-path-model.md) and [Choose the learner journey and evidence of learning](04-learner-experience-and-evidence.md).

### Evidence and validation

The Handbook is the initial course and content source for evaluating the experience. It does not restrict the platform's identity, subjects, or audience to backend engineering.

No specific reachable learner group has been established, and no verified evidence of learner difficulties has been collected. The need for structure, continuity, and visible progress remains a hypothesis expressed through the owner's product priorities. Evaluating the Handbook experience does not establish learner demand or educational effectiveness. Real-learner validation remains open in the map's Not yet specified section; recruitment, methods, and timing have not been decided.

### Future learning models

Instructor-led courses, live classes, and scheduled learning experiences belong to the long-term product vision and are not first-release requirements. Future expansion must remain possible, but no supporting first-release infrastructure or detailed later-phase behavior is specified by this decision. Their direction and prerequisites will be considered in [Define the first release and later product phases](06-first-release-and-phases.md).
