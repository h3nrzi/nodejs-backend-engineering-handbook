# Service eligibility and hosting routes for the Iran-based pilot operator

Research date: **2026-10-17**. All external sources below were retrieved on that date. Planning evidence for **Investigate service eligibility and feasible hosting routes for the Iran-based operator** (canonical ticket in the working-tree effort; not included on this throwaway research branch); no provider, architecture, budget, account creation, deployment, or launch is approved.

## Finding and evidence boundary

**A domestic managed route merits costing; a foreign managed route still has material qualification gaps.** Liara is a concrete domestic candidate with individual-account and identity-verification evidence. GitHub explicitly supports Iran-resident individual developers under its own license, which supports the established repository workflow. Vercel represents a foreign managed hosting route, but its eligibility for this particular operator/use remains unverified and no supported way to pay its paid plan is established. These are research dispositions, not selections; the supporting sources and limits follow.

Owner inputs: an individual resident in Iran operates a free, invitation-only adult learning pilot, initially around 5–10 learners; the owner handles accounts, expenses, providers, support, and personal-data requests. The owner reports Iranian bank accounts and domestic payment cards in their own name, with **no internationally supported payment method confirmed**. These statements establish neither particular identity documents nor an already verified provider account. The owner subsequently prioritized domestic managed candidates for investigation/costing, without selecting a provider. Geography remains open subject to verified law and explicit review of locations, access, and transfers; there is no additional Iran-only policy and no specific transfer is approved. Relevant release scope is recorded in [Define the first release and later product phases](../issues/06-first-release-and-phases.md#answer).

Method: used Context7 `resolve-library-id` before `query-docs` for Vercel, GitHub, and Liara; followed official provider documentation and current terms. Searched official OFAC material and retrieved official consolidated regulations. No account, transaction, provider contact, or Iran reachability test was performed. A successful documentation fetch from this research environment says nothing about learner access from Iran.

Terminology: **supported** means explicit public evidence supports the stated eligibility/capability, subject to the stated limits; **conditional** means a plausible route with named prerequisites; **unverified** means evidence is insufficient; **unsupported** means the stated route cannot presently be justified by evidence or available owner capability; **excluded** means outside this investigation or explicitly prohibited by the relevant rule. Unverified is not a finding of prohibition.

## Current U.S. authorization is specific, not a general hosting exemption

OFAC states that the May 17, 2024 amendment to **31 CFR §560.540 superseded General License D-2**. Cite the regulation rather than presenting D-2 as the current operative instrument. [OFAC FAQ 1110](https://ofac.treasury.gov/faqs/1110).

The official GPO bulk eCFR XML was retrieved on **October 17, 2026**; the applicable volume’s amendment date is October 5, 2026 (`ecor`), and the section source note is 89 FR 43313, May 17, 2024. Its §560.540(a)(1) expressly includes e-learning platforms and supporting cloud services. Paragraphs (a)(2)–(3) impose software classification/authorization conditions; (b)(1), (4), and (5) retain blocked-person and other sanctions/export-control limits. Crucially, (b)(3) excludes hosting for websites of commercial entities located in Iran. A free pilot operated by an individual is not automatically classified either inside or outside that exclusion. These are separate facts needing qualification for the exact activity and provider. [Official GPO eCFR Title 31 XML, §560.540](https://www.govinfo.gov/bulkdata/ECFR/title-31/ECFR-title31.xml); [official May 2024 rule and explanation](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/05/17/2024-10721/iranian-transactions-and-sanctions-regulations).

OFAC confirms fee-based as well as no-cost cloud services can qualify when supporting authorized communications activities. It also lists authentication and SSL-related software/services subject to relevant conditions. That supplies a possible legal basis, not a finding that this app or every dependency qualifies. [OFAC FAQ 1087](https://ofac.treasury.gov/faqs/1087).

Do not substitute permission to serve Iranian end users for eligibility of an Iranian contracting customer. FAQ 1088's due-diligence examples specifically concern providers' **non-Iranian customers** serving Iran. It also says §560.540 does not authorize importing or dealing in Iranian-origin software, including hosting it on a mobile app store. Whether the contemplated app, source/deployment activity, and provider arrangement require another authorization remains unverified; an e-learning label does not resolve software origin. GitHub's own specific license is not transferable to another host. [OFAC FAQ 1088](https://ofac.treasury.gov/faqs/1088).

Payment authorization and an available payment rail are different. FAQ 345 explains permitted payments have conditions, including routing and Iranian-account limitations; it does not make an Iranian domestic card internationally usable or compel a bank/processor to process a payment. [OFAC FAQ 345](https://ofac.treasury.gov/faqs/345).

Currency limit: direct eCFR HTML/API requests were inaccessible; the successful GPO bulk download supplies official consolidated text as retrieved, not a guarantee against a subsequent amendment. Its title contains several volume amendment dates, so no single title-wide current-through date is asserted here. Recheck rules, sanctions screening, and provider policy before contracting. This review does not clear all non-U.S. export/sanctions regimes or issue a legal opinion on the owner's activity.

## Data locations, backups, and hidden dependencies

**Liara:** its privacy policy says information is stored in Iran but permits global transfer/access for international operations and describes service-provider sharing and end-user traffic logging. That is not an Iran-only residency commitment or a complete named subprocessor/backup-location schedule. Obtain product-specific locations, retention and deletion details, access arrangements, and subprocessors if considering it. Its signup KYC affects the operator account; it does not establish that learners must supply national IDs. [Liara privacy policy](https://liara.ir/privacy-policy/).

Liara terms require customer-maintained backups. On the base/developer tier, insufficient credit can stop service immediately and trigger deletion after **24 hours**, including application/database backups, object-storage buckets, email, or VPS/snapshots as applicable. The terms also retain provider-account/financial records; that is distinct from deletion of a learner's records inside the hosted app. Treat credit monitoring, independent recoverable copies, and retention as operational requirements to evaluate. [Liara service terms, account deletion, backup, and suspension sections](https://liara.ir/terms/).

Managed PostgreSQL docs describe daily automatic and manual database backups. They do not in that page establish backup geography, full retention, or independence from provider/account failure; product backups cannot substitute for a tested recovery plan. [Liara PostgreSQL backup docs](https://docs.liara.ir/dbaas/postgresql/how-tos/create-backup/).

Liara permits **Iran or Germany build locations**, and documents package-download limitations in the Iran build environment. Runtime location does not establish where source, build context, images, or secrets are processed. A foreign build is a separate supply/data-transfer dependency to qualify, not a default workaround. [Liara build-location docs](https://docs.liara.ir/paas/details/build-location/).

**Vercel:** function-region choice does not confine all data: official compliance docs describe global processing and globally replicated infrastructure backups unavailable for customer recovery. Any app database's backup and deletion contract is separate. Current subprocessor details must be reviewed for the selected products rather than inferred from the function region. [Vercel Security & Compliance Measures, data location and backup sections](https://vercel.com/docs/security/compliance), [Vercel subprocessor entry point](https://vercel.com/legal/sub-processors).

**GitHub:** the privacy statement describes processing in the U.S. and other countries where GitHub/affiliates/subprocessors operate. Source, review metadata, and CI/build logs need their own data inventory. Planning implication: do not put learner records or production data in repository fixtures or build logs; repository availability does not settle app-data residency. [GitHub General Privacy Statement, international transfers](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement).

**DNS/TLS, deployment and email:** Liara documents DNS management and automatically renewed certificates; this is capability evidence, not qualification of a domain registrar, certificate supply chain, or actual renewal during disruption. [Liara DNS](https://liara.ir/products/dns/), [Liara certificate docs](https://docs.liara.ir/paas/domains/enable-ssl/). Its email service documents SMTP sending and incoming HTTP forwarding. A dedicated support email remains required, but the actual mailbox/client/forwarding destination and message retention need selection; a transactional email product alone does not demonstrate a usable support inbox. [Liara email product](https://liara.ir/products/email/). GitHub integrations, package registries, external fonts/scripts/images, and any future identity/email SaaS also need service-specific eligibility and access checks if selected. This is not a requirement to add each SaaS category.

## What remains before provider decisions

1. **Contracting/payment evidence:** confirm exact Liara individual KYC, supported domestic top-up method, owner-name matching, account recovery and chosen products. No identifiers need to enter the public research note. For any foreign alternative, obtain explicit eligibility for a truthful Iran-resident individual account and this app/software-origin scenario, plus a supported way to pay within provider/processor rules. An intermediary, false address, borrowed identity, or location masking is not a qualified route.
2. **Legal applicability:** resolve the commercial-entity hosting exclusion and Iranian-origin software questions for any relevant foreign supply chain; combine with **Investigate Iranian individual-operator and learner-data obligations** (canonical ticket in the working-tree effort; not included on this throwaway research branch). A domestic vendor does not by itself establish compliance with all laws or upstream supplier terms.
3. **Costing inputs:** cost the conditional domestic route against the approved scope, including app, persistence, domain/DNS/TLS where applicable, support email and independently recoverable backups. Record assumptions and expiry/deletion exposure. Do not assume a permanent free tier or a paid foreign upgrade path. The budget ticket owns numbers and approval.
4. **Data contract:** establish a minimal data inventory and specific runtime, build, database, backup, logging, support and email destinations; review retention/deletion, provider access, subprocessors, transfers and restore/export procedures. Public statements about Iran storage are insufficient for an Iran-only promise.
5. **Connectivity evidence later:** before operation, test the actual learner journey on representative Iranian fixed/mobile networks and devices, plus owner console/source/deployment access and support email. Include TLS/DNS and required assets/authentication, not just a home-page request. Record dates/networks/failures; do not claim domestic or foreign hosting guarantees reachability or outage resilience. No such tests occurred in this research.
