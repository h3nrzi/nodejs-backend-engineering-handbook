## Answer

Public primary evidence establishes relevant Iranian personal-data rules and a promulgated platform privacy directive, including a subsequent amendment. It does **not** settle this pilot's regulatory classification, the directive's application to an individual, or any infrastructure-specific retention or transfer duty. The approved individual operator remains the real arrangement to evaluate; incorporation, permission to launch, and a particular hosting location do not follow from this research.

The bounded facts are: an Iran-resident individual; free invitation-only adult access; self-paced learning; private account/completion records and support; no accredited qualification, competency assessment, payments, or separate operating team. Treating these facts as an exemption would require evidence that was not obtained.

## Verified instruments and limits

### Electronic Commerce Act: data purposes and individual requests

[WIPO Lex's statutory text](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/7711) is an international repository reproduction, **not** assurance of the current consolidated Iranian statute. Articles 1 and 2 describe electronic information exchange broadly and include natural persons. Article 58 prohibits processing the listed sensitive personal data without explicit consent. Article 59 requires stated purposes, necessary/proportionate collection and purpose-limited use, accurate/current records, access and correction/removal of incomplete or incorrect records, and a facility to request complete erasure subject to the rules. Articles 60–61 refer health records and other access/exceptions to further rules.

**Pilot interpretation, unresolved:** identifiers linked to a learner's progress or support history appear to be personal data, even when the course is free. Confirm how “in the context of electronic exchanges” applies here, plus any subsequent regulations. No fixed deletion-response deadline or universal record-retention duration was established from this text. Sensitive disclosures can also arrive in support messages.

The WIPO metadata gives adoption **2003-10-17** and entry into force **2003-10-24**, while its text ends with adoption **1382/10/17** and Guardian Council confirmation **1382/10/24** (different calendar dates). These were not reconciled with an authenticated Persian promulgation. The [Majlis Persian record](https://rc.majlis.ir/fa/law/show/93997) failed readable retrieval after an initial HTTP success; do not use WIPO's metadata as a verified Iranian commencement calculation. Its English article 71 also contains a cross-reference requiring Persian verification; no penalty conclusion is drawn here.

### Promulgated privacy directive, with amendment

The official [Dotic promulgation notice](https://dotic.ir/news/15750) links the [complete directive and covering letter](https://media.dotic.ir/uploads/org/2024/01/20/170576324339587400.pdf), downloaded and inspected. The attached text records adoption **1402/10/11**; its covering letter instead says **1402/10/25**. Both identify promulgation **107198, 1402/10/27**. This discrepancy is preserved, not silently corrected.

The preamble names nongovernmental **legal persons** and executive bodies; clause 1 addresses **all service providers** through cyberspace systems/platforms. Thus individual coverage requires interpretation. If applicable, clauses 1-1–1-4 require explicit consent and transparent purposes/collection methods, required-versus-optional fields, renewed consent and advance notice for policy changes, minimization, prompt online deletion subject to legal obligations, disconnected offline preservation for those obligations followed by destruction, and encryption of identifying information. Clause 3's identity-ecosystem alignment depends on the specified government mechanism becoming available; that trigger was not verified.

The [official amendment notice](https://dotic.ir/news/16127) and its [signed amendment](https://media.dotic.ir/uploads/org/2024/03/27/171154787078123200.pdf) were downloaded and visually reviewed. Adopted **1402/12/21**, promulgated **100018, 1403/01/05**, it changes clause 1's initial implementation window from **two to four months**. It does not change the scope wording. These are historical compliance windows, not a fresh grace period granted to this pilot. No later amendment was found in the targeted official search; completeness is unproven.

**Planning consequence:** retain privacy, deletion/preservation and encryption requirements as applicability questions with real source text. Do not assert either an individual exemption or an unconditional duty from the preamble alone. Do not turn the directive's legal-preservation exception into a blanket six-month archive for every learner record.

### National Data and Information Management Law

The official [promulgation notice](https://dotic.ir/news/13236) and [complete signed law](https://media.dotic.ir/uploads/org/2022/10/30/166713163351308900.pdf) were retrieved; scanned pages were visually reviewed. Adoption **1401/06/30**, Guardian Council confirmation **1401/07/20**, presidential promulgation **139271, 1401/08/07**. A separate effective date was not established.

Article 1 defines covered public bodies, sector-regulated providers, and national data. Article 5 and its note apply policy through covered bodies and sectoral licences/contracts; article 6 addresses security/confidentiality for covered bodies and sector-regulated providers. This is not evidence that all private progress records become a national database, nor a general localization obligation for this pilot. Whether a sectoral licence would bring this activity within its framework remains unresolved.

### A proposal is not an enacted requirement

[Dotic's personal-data protection proposal record](https://dotic.ir/news/10419), dated **1400/07/17**, identifies a parliamentary **طرح** received **1400/06/24**, under proposal news. This establishes proposal status in that record, not enactment or present legislative status. No enactment/commencement evidence for a comprehensive successor statute was obtained. Neither “Iran has no data law” nor “this proposal is current law” is supported. This research imports no GDPR obligation from an Iranian operator or learner market.
